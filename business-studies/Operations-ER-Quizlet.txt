DEFINE: Globalisation	"Globalisation refers to the removal of barriers of trade between nations… characterised by an increasing integration between national economies and a high degree of transfer of capital, labour, intellectual capital and ideas, financial resources and technology." Simpler: the increasing interconnection and interdependence of global businesses and markets.
BUILD (characteristics & features): Globalisation	• Affects every input: materials (cheaper globally sourced), labour (cheaper overseas), facilities (offshore factories/tech), customers (bigger markets, different cultures → customisation), competition (overseas cost advantages), finance (cheaper global funds). • Key terms: supply chain (range of suppliers + relationships) and global web (suppliers chosen on lowest cost, lowest risk, maximum certainty). • Process link: impacts inputs (global resources), transformation (4 V's, task design, sequencing, technology, layout) and outputs (global reach, customisation, distribution efficiency). • Answered through: global factors, SCM, outsourcing.
QANTAS evidence: Globalisation	• +220,000 international seats FY25; 2025 capacity of 7.1M seats is 2.5% above pre-COVID. • International revenue $8.7bn FY24; 70% of assets global-facing, 50% Asia-focused. • Competition: 62 international airlines in 2024 vs 52 in 2000; 70% of the 40 airlines serving Australia get home-government support — Qantas does not. • Oneworld: 900 destinations, 170+ countries; Emirates partnership (Dubai hub cuts costs).
DEFINE: Supply chain management	"SCM involves integrating and managing the flow of supplies throughout the inputs, transformation processes and outputs in order to best meet the needs of customers." (The definition itself is the process link — quote it.)
BUILD (characteristics & features): Supply chain management	• Three aspects: logistics, e-commerce, global sourcing. • Suppliers must deliver the right inputs at the best price, reliably, in the required quantity and quality. • Lead time (order → delivery) determines how flexible operations can be. • An effective supply chain must respond to demand changes — breaks in production waste costs.
QANTAS evidence: Supply chain management	• ~10,000 supply partners, ~$12 billion annual spend. • Direct fuel pipeline Kurnell refinery → Sydney Airport (750M litres storage); 22,500 sqm Mascot distribution centre. • 97% of sales online, ~70% of bookings via the app/site. • SAF sourced from London (BP) and California (20M litres p.a. from 2025 — FY25); all aircraft bought globally in USD.
DEFINE: Technology (influence)	"Technology is the design, construction and/or application of innovative devices, methods and machinery upon operations processes."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Technology (influence)	• Administrative level: planning tech (Gantt charts, CPA), office + software tech → "assist with organisation, planning and decision making to help control the operational processes." • Processing level: machinery, robotics, CAD, CAM, CIM → "improve accuracy, speed and efficiency of production and therefore improved outputs." • Adopting adequate technology is essential to stay competitive in a digital era — automates tasks, cuts costs, gives data-driven insights. • Answered through the technology strategy (leading edge vs established).
QANTAS evidence: Technology (influence)	• $230M digital/customer investment FY24; RFID baggage tracking cut mishandled bags 30% FY24 vs FY23. • 28 A321XLRs delivered 2025 (FY25) — ~17% less fuel, +15% passengers; 787-9 burns 20% less fuel per seat than the 747. • Constellation AI weather-tracking saves 2% fuel/yr; FlightPulse avoided 5.71M kg CO2 in year one. • 240 Sydney kiosks cut check-in times up to 90%; free international Wi-Fi rollout from late 2024.
DEFINE: Quality expectations	"Quality expectations refers to the influence that consumers have on a business, as their expectations for a product will determine the way in which that product is designed, created and delivered."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Quality expectations	• Goods: quality of design, fit for purpose, durability. • Services: professionalism, reliability, level of customisation. • Australian expectations are high — stringent standards, consumer protection laws, industry regulation. • Process link: shapes design, production and delivery → drives monitoring, control and improvement and output quality. Classic strategy pairing: quality management (control/assurance/improvement + TQM).
QANTAS evidence: Quality expectations	• OTP 80% in Q4 FY24 (Jetstar 74%); Q4 FY24 OTP +10 pts / NPS +22 pts vs Q2; domestic NPS +24 points FY24. • $100M lounge investment (42 domestic + 9 international) FY24. • 4-star Skytrax; #2 safest airline in the world 2025 (Airline Ratings). • The warning: customer trust fell 74% → 47% (2023→24) — unmet expectations punish reputation.
DEFINE: Cost-based competition	"Cost-based competition is derived from determining the breakeven point (TC = TR) and then applying strategies to create cost advantages over competitors."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Cost-based competition	• If a competitor cuts prices, operations must cut costs: cheaper inputs, better efficiency, smaller product range. • Five cost levers: eliminate waste, bulk-buy, high-volume output, automation, economies of scale. • The tension: cut costs without compromising quality — crucial in price-sensitive markets. • Process link: cheaper inputs + efficient/automated transformation + high-volume outputs → lower cost per unit.
QANTAS evidence: Cost-based competition	• Cut $5 billion over 6 years (cost base −20%); further $400M cost-out planned for 2025 (FY25). • Unit cost 9.73c/ASK FY24, down 4.5% from 10.19c. • Jetstar: 11 million fares under $100 sold FY24. • Cost structure: staff 26%, aircraft operating 23%, fuel 22%.
DEFINE: Government policies	"Government policies are methods used by government that encourage the operations function to be more innovative and competitive, as it ultimately produces more income for the nation."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Government policies	• Policy areas: taxation, WHS, public health, environment, employment relations, trade. • Protection = artificial advantage given to domestic industries; tariff/quota removal forces cost-cutting to compete. • Broad and indirect — vs legal regulation which is specific and compulsory. • Process link: changes the cost of inputs (fuel taxes, subsidies), rules of transformation (WHS, employment relations), demand for outputs (travel restrictions).
QANTAS evidence: Government policies	• Government blocked Qatar Airways' extra flights (2023–24) — de facto protection. • Domestic aviation fuel taxed 6c/L; Sydney Airport Curfew Act 1995 (11pm–6am, $550k fines) shapes scheduling. • Net-zero-2050 policy → Qantas secured 500M litres SAF p.a. from 2028. • ~$2.7bn COVID assistance (incl. ~$900M JobKeeper) kept operations alive.
DEFINE: Operations influences (umbrella)	"Influences on operations refers to the various internal and external factors that impact the way a business manages and conducts its operational processes."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Operations influences (umbrella)	• The seven + CSR: Globalisation, Technology, Government policies, Legal regulation, Environmental sustainability, Cost-based competition, Quality expectations + CSR ("GTG LECoQ + CSR"). • External factors present both threat and opportunity — the business must adjust. • Each influence should be paired with a strategy that "makes sense" (e.g. quality expectations → quality management).
QANTAS evidence: Operations influences (umbrella)	• One example each: globalisation (Oneworld 900 destinations) · technology (RFID −30% mishandled bags) · quality expectations (NPS +24 FY24) · cost-based competition (Jetstar 11M sub-$100 fares) · government (Qatar block) · legal (ACCC $100M penalty) · environment (net zero 2050) · CSR (45,000 free flights after Rex/Bonza).
DEFINE: Transformed resources	"The inputs which are changed or converted into something else, or acted upon by the operation to produce goods or services." The three: Materials, Information, Customers.
BUILD (characteristics & features): Transformed resources	• Materials: basic elements of production — raw materials (unprocessed) + intermediate goods (already manufactured) = "direct materials". • Information: knowledge from research/investigation/instruction — external (ABS) and internal (reports, feedback, KPIs); guides how other inputs are used. • Customers: their needs "drive" operations, their choices shape inputs/processes — and they are themselves transformed (physically, emotionally, psychologically).
QANTAS evidence: Transformed resources	• Materials: aviation fuel (~3.5 billion litres a year), in-flight meals. • Information: ECAM aircraft-monitoring memos + ACARS performance data on every flight; booking data. • Customers: 51.8M passengers FY24 literally transformed from city A to city B; 16.4M frequent flyers as repeat transformed customers.
DEFINE: Transforming resources	"The inputs that carry out the transformation process — used to transform other resources but not themselves part of the finished product." The two: Human Resources + Facilities.
BUILD (characteristics & features): Transforming resources	• Human resources: employees providing the skills, knowledge, capabilities and labour to convert materials into goods/services — "the most important asset"; well-trained, communicative HR is crucial. • Facilities: the plant and machinery used in operations — key decisions on location, design/process layout, number of facilities, capacity.
QANTAS evidence: Transforming resources	• HR: 27,000+ staff (93% Australia-based) — pilots, cabin crew, engineers, ground crew; growing to ~32,000 by 2033. • Facilities: 347-aircraft fleet, terminals, maintenance hangars (Brisbane/Sydney/LA), and the $20M Sydney simulator training centre (opened 2024, trains 4,500 pilots/crew a year).
DEFINE: Outputs	"Outputs are the finished goods or services produced by the operations function that have a value to customers greater than the cost of inputs." Profit requires output revenue > input expenditure.
BUILD (characteristics & features): Outputs	• Two subtler outputs: customer service ("how well a business meets and exceeds customer expectations" — one dissatisfied customer tells ~11 others) and warranties ("promises to correct any defects"). • The feedback loop: warranty-claim numbers signal issues inside the operations process → feeds monitoring, control and improvement. • Competition and Consumer Act 2010: outputs must match description, be fit for purpose, defect-free.
QANTAS evidence: Outputs	• Service guarantees instead of typical warranties: refunds/compensation for cancellations, $50–100/day baggage-delay compensation, up to $2,000 for lost bags. • NPS as the output KPI: domestic NPS +24 points FY24; Closed Loop Feedback program. • Qantas Freight revenue $1.2B FY24, +11% in 1H FY25.
DEFINE: Operations strategies (umbrella)	"The strategies that operations managers may use to achieve operations goals and broader business goals… An effective operations strategy will give a business a competitive advantage."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Operations strategies (umbrella)	• The nine ("POGO SQuINT"): Performance objectives, Outsourcing, Global factors, Overcoming resistance to change, SCM, Quality management, Inventory management, New product/service design & development, Technology. • All strategies relate to producing the good/service — each one should be linked to the process stage it improves (inputs, transformation, outputs).
QANTAS evidence: Operations strategies (umbrella)	• One example each: technology ($12bn fleet renewal) · outsourcing (1,700 ground handlers, ~$100M/yr) · inventory (JIT fuel + spare parts) · quality management (Kaizen 2024, A-checks) · new product (Project Sunrise, mid-2026) · global factors (60% domestic share H1 FY25 = economies of scale) · overcoming resistance ($1bn redundancies, $275M/yr training).
DEFINE: Performance objectives	"Performance objectives are goals that relate to particular aspects of the transformation processes" — quantifiable targets/KPIs that gauge and guide operational efficiency.
BUILD (characteristics & features): Performance objectives	• Quality — being right (design / conformance / service) • Speed — being fast (wait times, lead times) • Dependability — being on time (warranty claims / complaints) • Flexibility — being able to change • Customisation — more options (mass customisation) • Cost — being productive (minimise expenses) Mnemonic: "QueSaDela Football CClub".
QANTAS evidence: Performance objectives	• Quality: $12bn fleet renewal from 2024. • Speed: Project Sunrise cuts travel ~4 hours; 35-min turnarounds. • Dependability: OTP 80% + cancellations 2.5% (2024) vs 44%/6.7% (2022). • Flexibility: 28 A321XLRs in 2025 (FY25); COVID freight pivot ($1.32bn FY21). • Customisation: Classic Plus — 20M reward seats, +13% redemptions. • Cost: 9.73c/ASK FY24, −4.5%.
DEFINE: New product or service design and development	"Creating and introducing new offerings to meet market demands or technological advancements — researching customer needs, designing a product to fulfil those needs, testing it and then implementing it."
BUILD (characteristics & features): New product or service design and development	• Consumer approach (market research decides features/quality) vs capability approach (new technology enables advanced functionality). • Service design: explicit service (tangible — time, expertise, skill) vs implicit service (intangible feeling of being looked after); more standardisation = lower operating costs. • Design considerations = the process link: supply chain, quality, capacity, cost.
QANTAS evidence: New product or service design and development	• Project Sunrise: 12 A350-1000s, 19-hour non-stop Sydney–London/NY, launching mid-2026, peak spend $1.2bn into FY25–26; wellness zones, redesigned cabins. • A220 cabins (largest single-aisle windows, fast free Wi-Fi); 8 new 787-9 Dreamliners (20% less fuel); Perth–Paris route. • Classic Plus rewards launched FY24 — $120M, 20M+ reward seats, redemptions +13%.
DEFINE: Logistics	"The part of the supply chain that focuses on moving inputs, resources and outputs through the supply chain as quickly as possible, saving as much time at each point as possible."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Logistics	• Components: transportation, storage (secure holding of stock), warehousing (storage, protection, later distribution), distribution centres (short-term only), materials handling (loading/unloading — pallets, forklifts, containers), packaging. • Goal: "an efficient steady flow of material through the supply chain."
QANTAS evidence: Logistics	• Direct oil pipeline Kurnell refinery → Sydney Airport with 750M litres of storage. • 22,500 sqm Mascot distribution centre; JETS Transport Express. • Coordinates the physical inputs behind every flight: catering, baggage, fuel, crew positioning — which let Qantas absorb the 132% flying rebound FY23 vs FY22.
DEFINE: E-commerce and global sourcing	E-commerce: "the buying of inputs via the internet" (SCM context). Global sourcing: "businesses purchasing supplies or services without being constrained by location" — buying wherever best meets requirements.
BUILD (characteristics & features): E-commerce and global sourcing	• E-commerce B2B: e-procurement auto-reorders when stock runs low → prevents supply shortfalls. B2C: selling online removes intermediaries/wholesalers → cheaper prices. • Global sourcing upside: cheapest/best-quality inputs worldwide; downside: exchange rates, differing laws, longer lead times.
QANTAS evidence: E-commerce and global sourcing	• E-commerce: 97% of sales online; ~70% of bookings via qantas.com/app; 8.5M monthly website visits; TripADeal +60%. • Global sourcing: aircraft from Airbus/Boeing bought in USD; SAF from London (BP) and California (20M L p.a. from 2025 — FY25); offshore maintenance providers.
DEFINE: Quality management: control, assurance and improvement	"Quality management refers to the maintenance of consistency, reliability and sustainability to ensure the product meets the quality expectations of the customer." Three components: control, assurance, improvement.
BUILD (characteristics & features): Quality management: control, assurance and improvement	• Quality control (detection): monitoring + corrective action — feed-forward (before production = inputs), concurrent (during = transformation), feedback (final product = outputs). • Quality assurance (prevention): quality assured before reaching the market — proactive; ISO 9001 certification. • Quality improvement (continuous): continuous improvement + TQM (benchmarking, employee empowerment, customer focus, continuous improvement); Kaizen, Six Sigma. • Rugby: QA = game prep, QC = in-game adjustments, QI = reviewing the tape.
QANTAS evidence: Quality management: control, assurance and improvement	• Control: A-checks every 400–600 flight hours (200–300 flights); 2019 737 wing-crack inspections (33 checked, 2 grounded). • Assurance: IOSA registration + CASA benchmarks (200+ audits); ISO 9001. • Improvement: Kaizen adopted 2024; Lean Six Sigma; Skytrax recovered 24th → 14th (2025); cancellations 7.1% → 2.5% in 2022.
DEFINE: Global factors	"Global factors refer to the strategies a business can use in a global environment to improve the operations process." The four: global sourcing, economies of scale, scanning and learning, research and development.
BUILD (characteristics & features): Global factors	• Global sourcing: acquire inputs worldwide to cut costs / lift quality. • Economies of scale: unit costs fall as production scales. • Scanning and learning: watch global trends, implement them at home. • R&D: systematic work to improve products and processes. • Quotable link: "R&D improves processes; scanning and learning improves strategies."
QANTAS evidence: Global factors	• Scale: 60% of domestic passengers H1 FY25; flying hours +25% while unit costs −5.2%. • Scanning: Emirates alliance; Jetstar Asia in a region forecast for 50% aviation growth. • R&D: $50M mustard-seed SAF research; Constellation flight planning (with the Australian Centre for Field Robotics); Project Sunrise. • Sourcing: Airbus/Boeing aircraft; SAF from London/California.
DEFINE: Economies of scale	"The cost advantages a business obtains due to their scale of operation — as production increases, cost per unit falls, meaning profitability will rise."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Economies of scale	• Why: fixed costs (rent, machinery, aircraft, training infrastructure) are spread over more units + bulk-buying discounts. • Selling into the global market increases scale further. • Lower unit cost → cheaper COGS → higher gross profit → more competitive pricing. • Limit: only up to the technical optimum — beyond it, costs rise again.
QANTAS evidence: Economies of scale	• Group carried 60% of domestic passengers in H1 FY25 (FY25). • Total flying hours +25% (2023→24) while unit costs fell 5.2%. • Fixed costs — the $525M Sydney terminal lease, hangars, training centres — spread across ~820 daily flights. • ~$12bn procurement scale → better supplier deals.
DEFINE: Scanning and learning	"The systematic process of acquiring information about changes and trends in external economic conditions and using them to make decisions about operations. Scanning is looking overseas to find new business ideas and trends; learning is the implementation of this information by changing processes domestically."
BUILD (characteristics & features): Scanning and learning	• Constant watch on global trends, technological advancements and market shifts → adjust strategies and operations proactively. • Builds a culture of continuous improvement and adaptability, sustaining long-term growth. • Pairs naturally with benchmarking against world-leading competitors.
QANTAS evidence: Scanning and learning	• Emirates alliance — leveraging Emirates' Dubai hub and infrastructure learnings. • Jetstar Asia expansion into a region with 50% forecast aviation growth over 10 years. • NPS benchmarked against Emirates; watched global fleet trends → adopted fuel-efficient A321XLR/787 and cabin tech (Wi-Fi, IFE).
