## 1.0 — MARKET SEGMENTATION, PRODUCT/SERVICE DIFFERENTIATION AND POSITIONING

### THEORY paragraph

- **Definition (segmentation):** "Market segmentation occurs when the total market is subdivided into groups of people who share one or more common characteristics." The aim is to increase sales by better understanding and responding to the desires of different target groups.
- **Segmentation variables (name at least two):** **demographic** (age, sex, income, occupation, cultural background), **geographic** (location, region, city size, climate), **psychographic** (attitudes, values, beliefs, lifestyle, motives), **behavioural** (loyalty, usage rate, purchase occasion — i.e. *what the customer does*, not who they are).
- **Definition (product/service differentiation):** "the process of developing and promoting products that are distinguishable from competitors' offerings" — the purpose is to create a **competitive advantage** on points such as customer service, quality, convenience, value for money, environmental and social concerns.
- **Definition (positioning):** "the development of a product image and perception in the mind of the customer relative to competitors." The business must use elements of the marketing mix to shape and maintain that image. Positioning **sets the foundation for all other marketing strategies** — this is why segmentation → differentiation → positioning is a *sequence*, and running it in order is what makes the other four P's coherent.
- **Build:** segmentation reduces resource waste and the risk of profit loss because the marketing mix is aimed only at consumers likely to respond; differentiation gives the business more **leverage over price**; positioning converts that leverage into brand equity.

### LINK TO PROCESSES (be specific — name the exact stage)

- **Situational analysis** — segmentation is only possible after a **SWOT** identifies which segments the business has the internal strength to serve, and after the **product life cycle** stage is identified (maturity demands segmentation and differentiation to defend share; growth demands expanded distribution).
- **Market research** — segmentation *is* the application of market research: **primary data** (in-flight surveys, mail surveys, complaint monitoring, discussion with customer contact staff) and **secondary data** (government statistics, airline publications, competitor interviews) are what define the segments in the first place.
- **Identifying target markets (stage 4)** — this is the process stage segmentation directly *executes*. It determines whether the business runs a **mass**, **market segmentation** or **niche** approach, and which is the **primary market** (most marketing resources) versus the **secondary market**.
- **Developing marketing strategies (stage 5)** — positioning dictates the settings of every other element of the mix; a premium position forces premium price points, premium physical evidence and premium people.
- **Monitoring and controlling** — **market share analysis** (comparing sales performance against competitors) is the KPI that tells you whether the segmentation worked, and triggers **revising the marketing strategy** if a segment is lost.

### LINK TO INFLUENCES

- Answers **factors influencing customer choice** — psychographic and behavioural segmentation exist precisely because *psychological* (perception, motives, self-image) and *sociocultural* (social class, family and roles, reference groups) influences fragment a market.
- Answers **economic** influences — a downturn shifts consumers to the price-sensitive segment, so a business needs a brand positioned there ready to catch them.
- Constrained by **ethical** influences — segmentation must not slide into discriminatory targeting or **price discrimination** in its illegal forms.

### LINK TO MARKETING OBJECTIVES

- **Increasing market share** — a dual-brand structure defends the top and bottom of the market simultaneously.
- **Expanding the product range/mix** — differentiation is how new lines are justified.
- **Maximising customer service** — segments let service be tailored rather than averaged.

### QANTAS paragraph (6 stats)

Qantas applies **behavioural segmentation by trip purpose**, splitting cost-sensitive leisure travellers to Jetstar, premium and corporate travellers to Qantas mainline, and regional travellers to QantasLink — then differentiates each with a distinct marketing mix.

1. **Qantas' domestic market share collapsed from 80% to 65% after Virgin entered in 2000**, which is precisely why Jetstar was launched in **May 2004** as a competitive-positioning counter — the single best cause-and-effect stat in the topic.
2. The dual-brand structure now carries **60% of Australia's domestic passengers (H1 FY25)**, with Jetstar lifting domestic capacity **+8% in FY24** (**+15% group capacity**) and leisure earnings **+54% year-on-year**.
3. Qantas **offloads unprofitable leisure destinations such as Bangkok and the Gold Coast onto Jetstar**, retaining the route and the market share while shedding the cost base.
4. Differentiation is funded: **$230 million invested in customer experience in FY24**, including a **$100 million refresh of 42 domestic and 9 international lounges**, plus new **A220 cabins** with the largest windows of any single-aisle aircraft and 10 business seats in a 2-2 configuration.
5. Target-market positioning on the corporate segment: Qantas holds roughly **80% of the domestic corporate aviation market**, built on **Cityflyer** (launched 2001, Melbourne–Sydney every 30 minutes) — and **Sydney–Melbourne is the most lucrative route in the world, generating $1.9 billion for Qantas in H1 FY23 alone**.
6. Competitive positioning as Australia's flagship carrier is validated externally: a **2023 cancellation rate of 3.2% against an industry average of 3.7%**, and a ranking of **#2 safest airline in the world (AirlineRatings, 2025)**.

**Judgement:** *Based on the above criteria, Qantas has been astoundingly effective in its use of segmentation, differentiation and positioning — the strategy did not merely defend market share, it recovered a 15-point share loss and converted it into the most profitable route in global aviation.*

## 2.0 — PRICE (PRICING METHODS AND PRICING STRATEGIES)

### THEORY paragraph

- **Definition:** "Price is the amount of money a customer is prepared to offer in exchange for a product." Price is the only element of the mix that generates revenue — the other three cost money.
- **Pricing methods (set the base price):** **cost-based** (cost + mark-up; guarantees costs are covered), **market-based** (price set according to the level of demand and supply, and perceived value), **competition-based** (price set above, equal to or below competitors — undercutting wins volume but sacrifices margin and can damage the quality perception).
- **Pricing strategies (fine-tune the base price):** **price skimming** (a high initial price for a product with unique features, lowered as competitors catch up), **price penetration** (the lowest possible price to capture maximum market share and stimulate unsatisfied demand), **loss leaders** (a product sold below cost to attract customers who then purchase at full margin), **price points** (distinct price levels catering to different perceptions of value, widening the customer base).
- **Price and quality interaction:** "the perceived relationship between a product's price and its quality in the mind of the consumer" — high price signals high quality and reinforces a premium position, but **only if the service experience justifies it**; charging premium prices during a service failure is the fastest way to destroy brand equity.

### LINK TO PROCESSES (be specific)

- **Situational analysis** — the **product life cycle** dictates the pricing strategy: *introduction* suits skimming or penetration, *maturity* forces price competition and cost reduction, *decline* triggers harvesting.
- **Market research** — market-based pricing is impossible without demand data; competition-based pricing requires **secondary research** on competitor fares.
- **Establishing market objectives** — price is the lever that most directly moves the revenue and market share objectives, and it is the constraint on the load-factor objective (fares must be low enough to fill seats).
- **Implementation → developing a financial forecast** — pricing decisions feed directly into the estimate of expected revenues, costs and profits.
- **Monitoring and controlling** — **sales analysis** (sales volume by product/segment/territory) and **marketing profitability analysis** (profitability of each segment as a percentage of sales) are the two KPIs that test a pricing strategy; **revising the marketing strategy** is where fares get moved.

### LINK TO INFLUENCES

- Answers **economic influences** most directly — interest rates, inflation and cost-of-living pressure determine willingness and ability to spend, and travel is **discretionary expenditure**, making price the first lever pulled in a downturn.
- Answers **engaging in fair competition (ethical)** — competition-based pricing is legal, but **price fixing/collusion**, **cartel conduct**, **resale price maintenance** and **long-term loss leading to undercut smaller competitors** are illegal under the *Competition and Consumer Act 2010*.
- Answers **consumer laws** — **price discrimination** (different prices for the same product in separate markets without justification) and the requirement to display the true, all-inclusive price.

### LINK TO MARKETING OBJECTIVES

- **Increasing market share** — penetration and loss leaders buy share directly.
- **Maximising customer service** — price and quality interaction; premium pricing funds the service standard that justifies it.
- **Expanding the product range** — price points *create* the product range (Economy → Premium Economy → Business → First).

### QANTAS paragraph (6 stats)

Qantas uses **market-based pricing** as its method — fares are set by the level of demand and supply — then fine-tunes with a full suite of strategies across its two brands.

1. **Price penetration (Jetstar):** **11 million fares sold under $100 in FY24** (approximately 10 million in 2023 amid the cost-of-living crisis, alongside 5 million reward seats), sustaining volume in the price-sensitive segment.
2. **Price skimming (Qantas mainline):** a **20% premium charged on the non-stop Perth–London route**, with Project Sunrise one-way **first-class suites to New York estimated at $15,000** against **$6,000 business and $1,500 economy**.
3. **Loss leaders:** at Jetstar's launch Qantas released fares **as low as $19 on Sydney–Melbourne**, and offered **100,000 tickets at $49** plus a two-for-one deal to seize initial share.
4. **Price points:** four distinct classes — **Sydney–LA business exceeds $8,000 and first class reaches $20,000**, against roughly **$2,500** for a comparable domestic premium fare.
5. **Competition-based pricing in action:** when Rex entered the Brisbane–Melbourne route, **Virgin matched Rex's $79 fare while Jetstar undercut it at $65**; more broadly, Bonza's 2023 entry drove Qantas' **average revenue per passenger 13.5% lower in 2023 than 2022**.
6. **Price and quality interaction:** Qantas backed its premium pricing with a **$100 million lounge refresh** and the **Classic Plus program (April 2024, ~$120 million)** releasing **20 million more reward seats** and lifting redemptions **13%** — while **international yield fell 6.6% in 1H FY25** as capacity normalised, proving prices must track the market, not management's preference.

**Judgement:** *Qantas' pricing has proven highly effective because the dual-brand structure lets it occupy both ends of the price spectrum simultaneously — skimming $15,000 first-class suites while penetrating with 11 million sub-$100 fares — a range no single-brand competitor can replicate.*

## 3.0 — PROMOTION (THE PROMOTION MIX AND THE COMMUNICATION PROCESS)

### THEORY paragraph

- **Definition:** "Promotion refers to the methods used to inform, persuade and remind a target market about a business' products." It seeks to attract new customers, heighten awareness, increase sales and increase brand loyalty.
- **The promotion mix (name and define at least three):**
  - **Advertising** — "a paid, non-personal message communicated through a mass medium." Large reach, but expensive and one-way.
  - **Personal selling** — "the activities of a sales representative directed to a customer in an attempt to make a sale." Tailored and trust-building, but costly in wages.
  - **Relationship marketing** — "the development of a long-term, cost-effective relationship with individual customers." Loyalty programs convert one-off buyers into repeat revenue.
  - **Sales promotions** — "a direct inducement or incentive offered to customers to encourage a purchase" (coupons, discounts, samples). Short-term, cost-effective, good for stimulating demand troughs.
  - **Publicity and public relations** — publicity is "any free news story about a business"; public relations is "creating favourable relationships with customers and stakeholders," including crisis management.
- **The communication process:** **opinion leaders** — "a person who influences others' desires" (an actor, athlete or expert) — and **word of mouth** — "customers influencing each other during conversation." Word of mouth is the most trusted form because it is perceived as unbiased; it is also the most dangerous, because **one dissatisfied customer tells approximately 11 potential customers**.
- **Build:** an effective promotional strategy uses a *mix*, because each element reaches a different audience, builds a different kind of credibility, and hedges the risk of any one channel failing.

### LINK TO PROCESSES (be specific)

- **Market research** — promotion is only targeted if research has identified customer preferences, attitudes, brand preferences and buying intentions. **Net Promoter Score** monitoring is both research input and control KPI.
- **Situational analysis** — the **product life cycle** determines the promotional objective: *introduction* = awareness-building and educational advertising; *growth* = brand acceptance; *maturity* = brand loyalty and reminder advertising; *renewal* = leveraging existing brand equity.
- **Establishing market objectives** — promotion is the stage that converts abstract objectives ("rebuild brand and reputation") into measurable campaign targets.
- **Developing marketing strategies (stage 5)** — promotion must be consistent with the positioning established in stage 4, or the message contradicts the brand.
- **Implementation, monitoring and controlling** — the **financial forecast** must estimate promotional cost; **comparing actual and planned results** via **sales analysis** measures campaign return; **revising the marketing strategy** covers pulling or re-cutting a campaign that misfires.

### LINK TO INFLUENCES

- Answers **psychological and sociocultural** factors influencing customer choice — advertising works on perception, attitudes and self-image; opinion leaders and word of mouth work through **reference groups**.
- Constrained by **consumer laws** — **deceptive and misleading advertising** is prohibited under the ACL, covering fine print, bait-and-switch, exaggerated claims, distorted before/after images, unsupported "9 out of 10" surveys and country-of-origin claims.
- Constrained by **ethical influences** — **truth, accuracy and good taste in advertising**; good taste is highly subjective, so marketers must be alive to community sensitivities around race, gender and religion.

### LINK TO MARKETING OBJECTIVES

- **Increasing market share** — advertising drives awareness which drives trial.
- **Maximising customer service** — relationship marketing *is* a service objective delivered through a promotional tool.
- **Promoting ethical and socially responsible behaviour** — publicity and PR are how CSR becomes a marketing asset rather than a cost.

### QANTAS paragraph (6 stats)

Qantas runs the full promotion mix, anchored on emotive national-identity advertising and the single most valuable relationship-marketing asset in Australian business.

1. **Advertising:** the **"I Still Call Australia Home" campaign debuted in 2004 at a cost of $10 million** and was re-envisioned in 2022 with Kylie Minogue and Hugh Jackman — **domestic routes reached 110% of pre-COVID levels within two months of the relaunch**, a direct measurable return on a promotional spend.
2. **Advertising (current):** the **"Australia in the Sky" campaign (2024)** targets the North American market using Australian imagery, while **"Feels Like Home Again"** was praised for authenticity and genuine emotional response — evidence of *truth and accuracy* in advertising.
3. **Relationship marketing:** the **Qantas Frequent Flyer program reached 16.4 million members in FY24**, with **loyalty activity up 11%** and **membership up 14%**, delivering **$511 million underlying EBIT in FY24** and **$255 million in 1H FY25 alone**.
4. **Sales promotions:** Qantas discounted **more than 500,000 fares across its domestic network over a single 72-hour period**, with economy fares from **$109 one-way and over 25 routes priced under $150**, plus a **50% off points-seats promotion from October 2023**.
5. **Publicity and public relations:** a **30-year sponsorship of the Wallabies**, **$37.7 million raised with UNICEF's "Change for Good" over 21 years**, and **45,000 free flights offered to customers stranded by the Rex and Bonza collapses in 2024**.
6. **Opinion leaders and word of mouth:** **Hugh Jackman** serves as global brand ambassador, and Qantas leverages **1 million Instagram followers** plus **8.5 million monthly website visits** — with the outcome visible in a **domestic Net Promoter Score up 24 points in FY24** and **global NPS up 22 points in 2024**.

**Judgement:** *Qantas' promotion has been decisively effective — but its own case study proves the limit of the strategy: no volume of advertising offset the reputational damage of the ghost-flights scandal, with customer trust collapsing from 74% in August 2023 to 47% in June 2024, demonstrating that promotion can amplify a brand but cannot manufacture one.*

## 4.0 — FACTORS INFLUENCING CUSTOMER CHOICE (PEGS)

### THEORY paragraph

- **Definition:** "Customer choice refers to the decisions and actions of customers when they search for and purchase goods and services." Understanding these factors lets a business predict customer trends and maximise the effectiveness of its marketing strategies.
- **Psychological (intrinsic — internal, mental):** **perception** (the opinion consumers hold about a good or service), **motives** (the underlying reason for purchasing — safety, comfort, sustenance), **attitudes and beliefs** (overall feeling formed from experience, family, education), **personality and self-image** (people buy products that reflect how they see themselves), **learning** (information gained from past experiences).
- **Sociocultural (extrinsic — forces exerted by others):** **social class** (based on occupation, income, education — higher class means higher discretionary income), **culture and subculture** (shared values, beliefs, traditions), **family and roles**, **reference/peer groups** (primary = friends, frequent contact; secondary = celebrities, occasional contact).
- **Economic:** factors affecting a customer's **willingness and ability to spend**. In an **upturn/boom** — rising incomes, low unemployment, high consumer confidence, expenditure rises (and interest rates are raised to slow inflation). In a **trough** — high unemployment, low consumer spending (and interest rates are dropped to facilitate growth). Economic influences have a **more direct and immediate impact** on consumer choice than the other three.
- **Government:** **economic policies** (counter-cyclical fiscal and monetary policy affecting consumption), **laws and regulation** (age restrictions, taxes, subsidies), and government campaigns promoting or discouraging behaviours.

### LINK TO PROCESSES (be specific)

- **Situational analysis** — customer-choice factors populate the **O** and **T** of the **SWOT** (an economic upturn is an opportunity; a cost-of-living crisis is a threat) and determine the **product life cycle** position of a product.
- **Market research** — this influence is the *reason* market research exists. **Determining information needs** → **collecting primary data** (surveys, observation, experiment) and **secondary data** → **analysing and interpreting** to find patterns in buying behaviour.
- **Establishing market objectives** — objectives must be **attainable** given the economic environment; a recession forces objectives to be rewritten downward.
- **Identifying target markets** — psychographic and behavioural segments *are* psychological and sociocultural influences turned into market divisions.
- **Monitoring and controlling** — a shift in customer choice shows up as a variance in **sales analysis** and **market share analysis**, triggering **revising the marketing strategy**.

### LINK TO STRATEGIES

- **Segmentation, differentiation, positioning** — psychographic and behavioural segmentation are the direct response.
- **Price** — the direct response to economic influences (penetration pricing and loss leaders during downturns; skimming during booms).
- **Promotion** — the direct response to psychological and sociocultural influences (advertising to perception and self-image; opinion leaders and word of mouth to reference groups).
- **Product** — branding and packaging respond to perception and self-image; the **total product concept** (tangible *and* intangible benefits) exists because customers buy the intangible.

### QANTAS paragraph (6 stats)

1. **Psychological (perception):** Qantas' Net Promoter Score **doubled** in response to investment in operational reliability, with on-time performance rising to **74.1% in 2023 from 67.1% in 2022** and reaching **80% in FY24 (against 44% in 2022)** — proof that perception tracks operational fact.
2. **Psychological (attitudes and trust):** the counter-case — customer trust in Qantas **fell from 74% in August 2023 to 47% in June 2024**, and its **RepTrak reputation score, though recovering 12 points to 67/100 in FY24, remains well below its peak of 80/100** when it was Australia's most trusted brand.
3. **Sociocultural (family, reference groups):** Qantas launched **Classic Plus Flight Rewards in April 2024 at an estimated cost of $120 million**, releasing **20 million more reward seats** to address diverse family and leisure travel needs — with **redemptions rising 13%**.
4. **Sociocultural (peak demand from family roles):** demand peaks with school holidays — **4.7 million domestic aviation passengers in July 2022** — forcing capacity and pricing to follow the social calendar.
5. **Economic:** air travel is **discretionary expenditure**, making Qantas acutely cyclical — the **RBA's tightening cycle to a 4.35% cash rate cut first-half profits by 13%**, and industry international passenger revenue swung from **$610 billion (2019) to $189 billion (2020) and back to $648 billion (2023)**, dragging Qantas' own revenue from **$17.7 billion (2019) to $5.9 billion (2021) and up to $21.94 billion in FY24 (+10.7%)**.
6. **Government:** COVID border closures eliminated demand outright, requiring **approximately $2.7 billion in government assistance (including ~$900 million in JobKeeper)**; the government subsequently **blocked Qatar Airways' additional flights (2023–24)**, and its **net-zero-2050 commitment** pushed Qantas to target **10% SAF by 2030 and 60% by 2050**.

**Judgement:** *Qantas has responded effectively to factors influencing customer choice, converting a 27-point collapse in customer trust into a 24-point NPS recovery — but the 13% profit fall caused by a single interest-rate cycle demonstrates that economic influences remain largely beyond marketing's control, and can only be absorbed, not answered.*

## 5.0 — CONSUMER LAWS (WIPD)

### THEORY paragraph

- **Definition:** "The **Australian Consumer Law (ACL)** was introduced to protect consumers and provide uniformity of legal protection across all states." Its provisions sit within the **Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth)**, which has two purposes: (1) protect consumers against undesirable practices, and (2) regulate marketing and trade practices that restrict competition. It is enforced by the **ACCC** and replaced the *Trade Practices Act 1974*.
- **Deceptive and misleading advertising:** "creating a false impression to intentionally influence and deceive customers." Techniques the syllabus names: **fine print**, **bait-and-switch**, **before/after images** that are distorted, **unsupported tests/surveys** ("9 out of 10 people"), **country-of-origin** claims ("made in" vs "product of"), **exaggerated claims**, **vague statements** and false **special offers**. Misleading advertising is illegal, but "grey areas" and loopholes remain.
- **Price discrimination:** "setting different prices for the same product in separate markets." Mostly legal — quantity discounts and age-based discounts are permitted — but illegal where it is discriminatory or substantially lessens competition.
- **Implied conditions:** "the unspoken and unwritten terms of a contract of sale." Products must match their description, be **fit for purpose**, be **safe to use** and of **acceptable quality**.
- **Warranties:** "a written guarantee that faulty products will be repaired or replaced within a set period." Statutory warranties under the ACL apply regardless of any manufacturer's warranty, and a "no refund" sign is meaningless. Warranties both protect consumers and function as a **powerful marketing tool**.
- **Build (the evaluation angle):** compliance imposes cost, but non-compliance imposes penalty **plus reputational damage** — and reputation is the most significant intangible asset a business owns.

### LINK TO PROCESSES (be specific)

- **Situational analysis** — legal exposure is a **weakness** and regulatory tightening a **threat** in the **SWOT**; ongoing lawsuits and parliamentary inquiries belong in the W quadrant.
- **Establishing market objectives** — objectives must be **relevant** and lawful; an objective to maximise yield cannot be pursued through undisclosed charges.
- **Developing marketing strategies** — this is where consumer law bites hardest: it constrains **promotion** (advertising claims must be substantiated) and **price** (the displayed price must be the true price).
- **Implementation, monitoring and controlling** — compliance auditing sits inside monitoring; **revising the marketing strategy** is the mechanism through which a business corrects an unlawful practice — including issuing refunds and re-displaying prices.
- **Market research** — **sugging** (selling under the guise of research) is where consumer law reaches into the research stage itself.

### LINK TO STRATEGIES

- **Promotion** — advertising must be truthful and substantiated; this constrains every element of the promotion mix.
- **Price** — pricing methods must not amount to price discrimination, and displayed prices must be all-inclusive.
- **Products (branding and packaging)** — packaging claims fall under deceptive and misleading conduct; warranties are part of the **total product concept**.
- **E-marketing** — online booking flows are exactly where hidden charges and misleading availability claims arise.

### QANTAS paragraph (6 stats)

1. **Deceptive and misleading advertising — the flagship case:** the ACCC established that Qantas **sold tickets on more than 8,000 flights it had already cancelled, continuing to sell them for up to 47 days after cancellation**, affecting approximately **880,000 customers**.
2. **The penalty:** Qantas agreed to a **$100 million civil penalty plus $20 million in refunds (ordered October 2024)**, with compensation paid to over **86,000 directly affected customers**.
3. **A repeat offender, not a one-off:** Qantas was **fined $2 million in 2019 for misleading Jetstar customers about their refund rights** when flights were delayed or cancelled.
4. **Price and disclosure:** following ACCC legal action, **Qantas now publishes the true cost of fares, including extra charges and levies it had previously hidden** from the displayed price.
5. **Fair competition and price fixing:** Qantas was **fined $61 million in 2007 and a further $20 million in 2008** for colluding with other airlines to fix fuel surcharges on freight services — cartel conduct under the CCA.
6. **The compliance response:** Qantas established a **dedicated Australian Consumer Law webpage**, instituted **policy audits by the Qantas Group Audit and Risk team**, **scrapped the 1 January 2024 expiry deadline on COVID flight credits in August 2023** after sustained criticism, and now guarantees **$50–100 per day compensation for delayed baggage** and compensation for flights over six hours late or cancelled.

**Judgement:** *Qantas' response to consumer law has been reactive rather than preventative and therefore only partially effective — the $120 million ghost-flights settlement and the collapse of customer trust from 74% to 47% demonstrate that the reputational cost of non-compliance vastly exceeded the revenue the practice generated.*

## 6.0 — ETHICAL INFLUENCES (TAPES)

### THEORY paragraph

- **Definition:** "Ethical behaviour in marketing refers to conducting promotional and advertising activities in an honest, transparent and moral manner." It involves **conduct that goes beyond legal requirements** — actions taken to behave as a responsible corporate citizen.
- **Truth, accuracy and good taste in advertising:** truth means "presenting factual information without deception"; **good taste is highly subjective**, so marketers must remain aware of community sensitivities around race, gender and religion.
- **Products that may damage health:** businesses must provide health warnings on potentially unsafe goods, and restrictions apply to cigarettes, alcohol and gambling.
- **Engaging in fair competition:** the CCA requires businesses to compete fairly. Prohibited: **price fixing/collusion**, **cartel conduct**, **resale price maintenance**, and **long-term loss leading** used to undercut smaller competitors.
- **Sugging:** "selling under the guise of a survey" — raises issues of invasion of privacy and deception.
- **Build (the evaluation angle, and where the marks are):** ethical marketing builds customer trust, positive reputation and contributes to CSR — *"if consumers see a business as unethical they won't buy from them."* But it carries **higher costs** (sustainable materials, longer and more transparent advertising, ethically vetted suppliers) and is **difficult** to execute, which can reduce short-term competitiveness. The temptation to act unethically arises **in the absence of law** — the classic examples being **greenwashing** and clinically ineffective products that remain legal to market. Voluntary codes fill the gap: the **AANA Code of Ethics (2012)**, enforced by the Advertising Standards Bureau.

### LINK TO PROCESSES (be specific)

- **Situational analysis** — ethical reputation is a **strength** or **weakness**; sustainability initiatives are an **opportunity**; consumer backlash is a **threat**.
- **Market research** — **sugging** is the ethical constraint on the data-collection stage; research must make its purpose clear and collect only relevant data.
- **Establishing market objectives** — **"promoting ethical and socially responsible behaviour" is itself a marketing objective** in the syllabus, which makes this the tightest influence-to-process link available.
- **Developing marketing strategies** — ethics constrains what claims may be made and which suppliers may be promoted (an ethical claim is only as strong as the weakest supplier behind it).
- **Monitoring and controlling** — reputation metrics (NPS, RepTrak, trust surveys) are the control KPIs, and a reputational failure triggers **revising the marketing strategy**.

### LINK TO STRATEGIES

- **Promotion (publicity and public relations)** — CSR only converts into marketing value if it is communicated; PR is also the crisis-management tool when ethics fail.
- **Positioning** — a values-based position ("Spirit of Australia", environmental leadership) is an ethical claim that must be continuously earned.
- **Price** — fair competition constrains loss leading and collusion; charging premium prices during service failure is an ethical, not just commercial, misjudgement.
- **Global marketing** — customisation must be culturally sensitive; standardisation risks giving offence in markets whose norms differ.

### QANTAS paragraph (6 stats)

1. **Environmental CSR:** Qantas committed to **net zero by 2050** with a **25% emissions cut by 2030**, backed by a **$400 million Climate Fund** and a **$290 million partnership with Airbus** to establish an Australian SAF industry — plus a **PR partnership with Jet Zero Australia and LanzaJet** to build Australia's first SAF plant, targeting **up to 100 million litres annually**.
2. **Waste and single-use plastics:** Qantas has eliminated **more than 200 million single-use plastic items since 2019**, cutting landfill waste by approximately **75%**, and recycled **2,842 tonnes of waste in FY22-23**.
3. **Social CSR:** the **UNICEF "Change for Good" partnership has raised $37.7 million over 21 years** supporting children in over 190 countries, alongside a **Reconciliation Action Plan (2021)** and support for the Great Barrier Reef and the Katingan Mentaya forest conservation project.
4. **Ethical promotion in a crisis:** after the **Rex and Bonza collapses in 2024**, Qantas offered **45,000 free flights** to stranded customers — a voluntary act beyond any legal obligation, and textbook ethical PR.
5. **Engaging in fair competition — the failures:** Qantas was **fined $61 million (2007) and $20 million (2008)** for fuel-surcharge collusion, was **accused of ambush marketing at the 2000 Olympics** despite not being an official sponsor, and was accused by the ACCC of anti-competitive conduct by **increasing seat capacity well beyond passenger demand** to squeeze rivals.
6. **Good taste and reputation:** Qantas was publicly criticised for using a photograph of a **10-year-old Aboriginal girl living in "undesirable conditions"** in its marketing, and more recently **criticised for charging high fares despite service failures and record profits** — with **customer trust falling from 74% to 47%** as the measurable consequence.

**Judgement:** *Qantas' ethical marketing has been effective in intent but inconsistent in execution — a $400 million Climate Fund and 45,000 goodwill flights sit alongside $81 million in collusion fines and a 27-point collapse in trust, illustrating the syllabus principle that ethical conduct must exceed legal compliance, not follow it.*

# 7. MARKETING PROCESSES — THE SPECIFIC POINTS BANK

*"The marketing process is the series of steps a business undertakes to market its products to consumers effectively." It must be completed in order.*

### 7.1 Situational analysis
- **SWOT** — "a strategic tool that evaluates an organisation's Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats." Internal (S/W — the business controls these) vs external (O/T — it does not).
- **Product life cycle** — **introduction** (slow sales, low/negative profit, awareness-building), **growth** (rapid sales, rising profit, new competitors enter, expand distribution), **maturity** (sales plateau, market saturated, intense competition → segmentation and brand loyalty), **decline** (harvesting or divesting), **renewal** (rejuvenating a declining product to start a new growth phase).
- *Qantas:* **S** — 16.4M loyalty members, globally recognised brand, alliances (Oneworld, Emirates), 26 countries and ~820 daily flights. **W** — average fleet age 15 years vs Emirates' 10, thin balance sheet, union disputes, lawsuits. **O** — A321XLRs unlocking new long-range routes, SAF, loyalty expansion. **T** — economic downturns, fuel prices, a weaker AUD, **Qatar Airways taking a 35% stake in Virgin Australia**. **PLC** — domestic and international are in **maturity**; **Jetstar Group is in growth**.

### 7.2 Market research
- **Three steps:** determining information needs → data collection → data analysis.
- **Primary data** — specially collected from original sources: survey, observation, experiment.
- **Secondary data** — previously collected for another purpose; internal or external.
- *Qantas:* **primary** — ongoing in-flight surveys, mail surveys, complaint monitoring, discussions with customer contact staff. **Secondary** — government statistics, airline publications, interviews with competitor executives. **Outcome** — research identified customer "pain points" that justified the **$230 million customer experience investment (Sept 2023, across FY23-24)**, and improved food and beverage offerings; **domestic NPS rose more than 110% by December 2023**.

### 7.3 Establishing market objectives
- **Definition:** "realistic and measurable goals to be achieved throughout the marketing plan" — must be **SMART** (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time-bound).
- **The four syllabus objectives:** increasing **market share**, expanding the **product range/mix**, maximising **customer service**, promoting **ethical and socially responsible behaviour**.
- *Qantas' actual objectives (memorise 3):* build **two complementary airlines** — Qantas (premium) and Jetstar (low fares); maintain a **combined domestic market share of 65%**; **sustain loads around 80%** by matching capacity with demand; restore **international capacity to 100% of pre-COVID**; **rebuild brand and reputation**; **align Qantas and Jetstar with Asia's growth**.

### 7.4 Identifying target markets
- **Definition:** "a group of present and potential customers which a business intends to sell its products to."
- **Three approaches:** **mass** (large undifferentiated market), **market segmentation** (a specific section sharing common needs), **niche** (a narrow segment of an already segmented market).
- **Primary market** = the segment most marketing resources are directed to; **secondary market** = smaller and less influential.
- *Qantas:* behavioural segmentation by trip purpose — **corporate/business travellers as the primary market (~80% of the domestic corporate market)**, cost-sensitive leisure to Jetstar, regional to QantasLink; **>50% of capacity directed to Asia**, targeting **50% forecast regional aviation growth over ten years**.

### 7.5 Developing marketing strategies
- The **marketing mix**: Product, Price, Promotion, Place + People, Processes, Physical evidence. Emphasis on each element varies with the marketing objectives and the stage of the product life cycle.
- *Qantas:* **Product** — Cityflyer; **Price** — yield adjustment (international yield −6.6%, 1H FY25); **Promotion** — 50% off points seats (from Oct 2023); **Place** — route expansion and the Qantas Distribution Platform.

### 7.6 Implementation, monitoring and controlling
- **Implementation** — executing the plan; requires **developing a financial forecast** (estimating expected revenues, costs and profits from projections of sales, expenses and pricing).
- **Monitoring** — **comparing actual and planned results** through three named tools: **sales analysis** (breaking aggregate sales down by product, segment and territory), **market share analysis** (comparing sales performance to competitors), **marketing profitability analysis** (profitability of each product/segment/territory as a percentage of sales).
- **Controlling** — **revising the marketing strategy**: taking corrective action, including changes to the mix, new product development and product deletion.
- *Qantas' corrective actions — the best "controlling" evidence in the topic:* introduced **Jetstar in May 2004** to counter Virgin's growing share; **lowered prices, cut flight frequencies and delayed aircraft orders during the 2009 GFC**; **cancelled orders and exited poor-performing routes in 2014**; **cut Hong Kong flights (2019–20)** after protests collapsed tourist traffic; **grounded aircraft and stood down staff (2020–23)**; **raised airfares to match fuel price rises** driven by the Ukraine war; **scrapped COVID credit expiry deadlines (Aug 2023)**; **added Classic Plus with 20 million reward seats (April 2024)** to restore the brand.

# 10. SWAP-IN STRATEGIES (if you need a fourth, or the question names one)

### 10.1 Place/distribution
- **Theory:** distribution channels (**direct** — producer to consumer; **indirect** — via an intermediary); **channel choice** (**intensive** — as many outlets as possible; **selective** — a moderate proportion; **exclusive** — one outlet in a large geographic area); **physical distribution issues** — transport, warehousing, inventory.
- **Qantas (6 stats):** **97% of total sales are online**; **70% of domestic bookings and 35% of international bookings** made online with **8.5 million monthly website visits**; **Jetstar sells 90% of tickets online**; indirect channels through **Flight Centre and American Express**, supported by a **Computer Reservations System** supplied to agents; the **Qantas Distribution Platform** built on IATA's New Distribution Capability standards; owned retail through **Qantas Holidays, Viva, Jetset, Jetabout and TripADeal** (**bookings +60%**), reducing agents' incentive to sell competitor tickets; **+100 new call centre agents across 98 locations**.

### 10.2 E-marketing
- **Theory:** "the practice of using the internet to perform marketing activities" — website, app, email, social media, SEO, PPC, content marketing, geo-targeting. **Advantages:** large reach, flexibility, increased ability to collect data, inexpensive. **Disadvantages:** may mislead, security and privacy concerns, less control over customer feedback and criticism, dependence on technology.
- **Qantas (6 stats):** **8.5 million website visits per month (2024)**; **1 million Instagram followers** plus presence across Facebook, LinkedIn, X, YouTube and TikTok; **monthly emails to Frequent Flyers** with targeted flight and accommodation deals; **tens of millions of the $230 million customer investment** directed into the Qantas App; the app launched with **baggage tracking in 2023**, cutting **mishandled bags 30% in FY24**; **240 self-serve kiosks** at Sydney Domestic cutting check-in times by up to **90%**.

### 10.3 Global marketing
- **Theory:** **global branding** (a uniform world image — provides consistency, improves awareness, cuts design and advertising costs); **standardisation** (uniform products worldwide — economies of scale, but ignores cultural preference); **customisation** (adjusting products for cultural differences — higher potential profit and market share, but less cost-effective); **global pricing** (customised, market-customised, or standard worldwide price); **competitive positioning** (how the business differentiates in a foreign market).
- **Qantas (6 stats):** the **Flying Kangaroo and "Spirit of Australia"** operate as a single global brand across all markets; **standardisation through the Oneworld alliance**, whose name and logo on member aircraft and tickets extends reciprocal frequent flyer, lounge and freight cooperation; **customisation via Jetstar Asia (e.g. Jetstar Japan)** with a refreshed name, culturally sensitive cabin layouts, Asian menus and language-fluent crew; **global pricing** — a one-way Sydney–London economy fare of **$1,200 against $800 Sydney–Tokyo (2023)**; **>50% of capacity directed toward Asia**, targeting **50% regional aviation growth over ten years**; the **"Australia in the Sky" campaign (2024)** customised for the North American market.

### 10.4 People, processes and physical evidence
- **Theory:** **people** — "the quality of interaction between the customer and the service provider"; **processes** — "the flow of activities a business follows in delivering a service"; **physical evidence** — "the environment in which the service is delivered," which must align with the positioning.
- **Qantas (6 stats):** **$275 million per year on staff training** and **2 million training hours delivered in 2023**; a **$100 million new training facility** (with a $20 million Sydney centre training 4,500 pilots and cabin crew a year); staff rated **4.5/5 on Skytrax**; processes — a **new group boarding system** ordering by seat location rather than ticket type, plus **240 check-in kiosks** cutting check-in time up to **90%**; physical evidence — **$100 million lounge refresh (42 domestic, 9 international)** and **Martin Grant-designed uniforms (2013, 2016)**, with **gender-based uniform rules removed in 2023**; new **A220 cabins** with advanced in-flight entertainment and improved comfort.
