# The science behind the 3-Day Coach

This game's **3-Day Coach** isn't guesswork — it implements the techniques that cognitive-science research has repeatedly shown to produce **durable verbatim recall** in a short window. This note summarises what the evidence says and how each finding maps to a feature.

## The core method: Successive Relearning
The single best-supported recipe for getting something memorised *and keeping it* is **successive relearning** — combining **retrieval practice** with **spacing**, practising each item until you can recall it correctly to a criterion (~3 correct recalls), then doing that again across several **spaced** sessions.
- Rawson & Dunlosky (2022), *Successive Relearning: An Underexplored but Potent Technique…*, **Current Directions in Psychological Science**. Recommends recalling each item to a criterion of ~3 correct, relearned across multiple spaced sessions; produces large, durable gains in real courses.
→ **Feature:** every line (“chunk”) is tracked individually; you must recall it correctly ~3× across spaced sessions before it counts as *mastered*.

## The ingredients

| Finding | Source | How the Coach uses it |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrieval practice / testing effect** — recalling beats re-reading, especially on delayed tests. | Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Test-Enhanced Learning*, **Psychological Science**. | Every step asks you to *produce* the line from memory, not just read it. |
| **Spacing effect** — distributing study beats massing; the optimal gap is ~10–20% of the retention interval (so for a ~3-day horizon, gaps of a few hours, plus overnight). | Cepeda, Vul, Rohrer, Wixted & Pashler (2008), *Spacing Effects in Learning: A Temporal Ridgeline of Optimal Retention*, **Psychological Science**. | Reviews are scheduled at **expanding intervals** (~10 min → 30 → 2 h → 8 h → next day) and the Coach nudges you to rest between sessions. |
| **Read–Recite–Review (3R)** — read, recite from memory, review; beats rereading and note-taking. | McDaniel, Howard & Einstein (2009), *The Read-Recite-Review Study Strategy*, **Psychological Science**. | New lines use exactly this 3-step flow. |
| **Production effect** — reading **aloud** is remembered better than reading silently. | MacLeod et al.; Ozubko et al. — production-effect literature. | The Coach explicitly prompts you to **say each new line out loud**. |
| **Errorful generation + feedback** — attempting recall (even failing) then seeing the answer beats pure study. | Kornell, Hays & Bjork (2009), *Unsuccessful Retrieval Attempts Enhance Subsequent Learning*, **JEP:LMC**. | You always attempt the line first; the marked answer is immediate feedback. |
| **Chunking & chaining** — small units (within working-memory limits), rehearsing the *transitions* between them (where people forget). | Working-memory / chunking literature; back-chaining. | The essay is split into ~65 short lines; recall cues show the **end of the previous line** to drill the join. |
| **Interleaving** — mixing items/topics beats blocking for long-term retention (a “desirable difficulty”). | Interleaving literature (e.g. Rohrer & Taylor). | Due reviews are pulled **across paragraphs**, not one paragraph at a time. |
| **Overlearning + sleep** — extra correct practice past first success adds durability; sleep between sessions consolidates memory and roughly halves the practice needed. | Overlearning literature; sleep-and-memory consolidation research. | Day 3 is timed full **exam write-outs**; the plan is spread across **3 nights** and re-tests prior days each morning. |

## The 3-day plan
- **Day 1 — Encode & first retrieval.** Introduce every line via Read–Recite–Review (aloud), then immediately recall it with feedback to a first correct.
- **Day 2 — Spaced retrieval, chaining & interleaving.** Re-test Day-1 lines (post-sleep), rehearse sentence joins, interleave paragraphs; progressively harder cues.
- **Day 3 — Overlearn & simulate.** Full first-letter recitation, then repeated **timed exam write-outs** of the whole essay, drilling any misses.

## Honest caveats
- 3 days is enough to get to confident verbatim recall **if** you do several short spaced sessions per day and sleep properly — but spacing means you can't cram it all into one sitting; the Coach enforces gaps on purpose.
- The production effect helps *memory for wording*, not comprehension — understanding the essay's argument still matters, which is why **Study** mode foregrounds the structure.
